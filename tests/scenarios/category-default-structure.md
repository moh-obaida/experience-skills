# Scenario: Challenge a Category-Default Structure Once

## Scenario
A team is designing a greenfield field-research product for ecologists who record species
observations, compare them with habitat conditions, revisit them later, and prepare reports. The
core product mechanics and audience are known, but information architecture, visual direction, and
screen composition are open. The first proposed concept is a familiar business dashboard with a
sidebar, top bar, KPI cards, and a table. The team asks whether to keep it or explore another
structure.

## Prompt
Help choose the product's primary working-screen structure. Derive the core job and instrument
from the brief. Challenge the default once with one materially different, usable interpretation;
compare them on job fit, clarity, instrument dominance, state/return behavior, and mobile use. Keep
the familiar dashboard if it serves the product better. Give a short rationale and state how the
choice should carry into one supporting route.

## Current problem
A familiar business-software pattern can win by default before the repeated field-to-review job is
considered. The opposite failure is novelty for its own sake or an overbuilt multi-concept design
exercise. The product needs a structural decision with a clear reason and a usable path across
routes.

## Expected skills
- experience-architect
- visual-identity
- composition-repair

## Key principles expected
- Begin with the repeated job and derive the working instrument, supporting context, and route
  structure; do not map product concepts directly into dashboard panels.
- Because design space is open and the initial layout is a category default, consider one
  structurally different challenger. Compare silhouettes and grayscale hierarchy, not only colors,
  fonts, or decorative treatments.
- Keep whichever structure best supports the job. The challenger is a test, not a novelty quota.
- Carry visual identity across the working screen and supporting route while allowing their
  compositions to differ by task.
- Preserve realistic observation capture, return, review, and reporting behavior on mobile.
- Keep the recommendation compact; do not fan out across every specialist or produce a full design
  system document.

## Unacceptable recommendations
- Automatically approving the sidebar, KPI cards, and table because the product is business
  software.
- Automatically rejecting that layout because it is familiar.
- Offering three or more directions or a theme/color-only alternative.
- Inventing an account gate, workflow stage, or mandatory map-first policy.
- Requiring identical page layouts to achieve cross-route coherence.
