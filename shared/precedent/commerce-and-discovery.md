# Precedent: Commerce and Discovery Surfaces

How product catalogs, stores, and marketplaces balance storytelling with buying and browsing.
Observed 2026-09-24; IDs refer to `research/observations/`.

---

### IKEA — filters and compare
`IK1 IK2 IK3` · https://www.ikea.com/us/en/ · rendered
- **What works:** plain Sort/Size/Color filters, compare on every card, location context in the header,
  products on flat light backgrounds.

### Steam — capsules with decision data
`ST1 ST2` · https://store.steampowered.com/ · rendered
- **What works:** dense category nav, featured carousel, price and reviews on each capsule.

### Apple — lineup as navigation
`AP2 AP3` · https://www.apple.com/iphone/ · rendered
- **What works:** a horizontal model navigator at the top; each model panel pairs "Learn more" with
  "View pricing".

### Aesop — narrative, then products
`AE1` · https://www.aesop.com/ · rendered
- **What works:** an editorial story introduces a collection; curated sets follow.
- **Right when:** a considered, sensory purchase where story adds value.

### Nike — campaign on top of efficient structure
`NK1` · https://www.nike.com/ · rendered
- **What works:** a campaign hero with two actions over dense navigation and search.

### Patagonia — values before products
`PA1 PA2` · https://www.patagonia.com/home/ · rendered
- **What works:** activism campaign first; products below.
- **Right when:** the brand's values are the reason customers choose it.

### PlayStation — carousel index as content
`PS1 PS2` · https://www.playstation.com/en-us/ · rendered
- **What works:** hero switcher thumbnails double as a preview of featured games.

### Coursera — marketplace columns
`CO1 CO2` · https://www.coursera.org/ · rendered
- **What works:** dense columns with provider logos and ratings.

### Booking.com — task row
`B2 B3` · https://www.booking.com/ · rendered
- **What works:** single-row search with a work-travel checkbox.
- **What does not:** a generic four-icon benefits row below.

### Airbnb — search-first
`ABd1` · https://www.airbnb.com/ · rendered
- **What works:** Where/When/Who as the focal element with photography rows.

### Wise — the product as the hero
`W1` · https://wise.com/ · rendered
- **What works:** a working quote instead of marketing art.

---

## Summary rules

1. Keep the path to price, availability, and the buy action short and visible; put story around it.
2. Put decision attributes (price, rating, availability) on list items.
3. Persistent context (location, store) in the header.
4. Story-first only when the purchase is considered and the brand story is the reason to buy.
