# Precedent: Identity Derived From the Product

Identity carriers that come from what the product *is* (its mechanic, content, object, or voice)
rather than from a trend. Observed 2026-09-24; IDs refer to `research/observations/`.

**How to use this file.** When choosing identity carriers, look for the equivalent source in your
product: its core mechanic, its signature object, its content type, its documentation, its data.

---

### Raycast — the keyboard
`RC1` · https://www.raycast.com/ · text
- **Source:** the core mechanic (keyboard-first launcher) is the hero visual.
- **Lesson:** show the interaction the product is built around.

### Brilliant — the lesson problem
`BR1` · https://brilliant.org/ · rendered
- **Source:** the product's own content type (an interactive geometry problem) is the hero image.
- **Lesson:** a sample of real content can be more distinctive than any illustration.

### Codecademy — the editor
`CC2` · https://www.codecademy.com/ · rendered
- **Source:** code editor conventions (slash, cursor, highlight) applied to the headline.
- **Right when:** the audience recognizes the tool's conventions.

### Framer — the prompt composer
`FR1` · https://www.framer.com/ · rendered
- **Source:** the product's actual input (AI prompt composer) is the hero's focal object.
- **Lesson:** for tools, the interface you will use can be the brand image.

### Wise — the quote
`W1` · https://wise.com/ · rendered + text
- **Source:** the transfer quote (amount, rate, fee, arrival) is the hero.
- **Lesson:** when the product's output is a number people care about, show that number working.

### Monzo — the coral card
`MZ1` · https://monzo.com/ · rendered
- **Source:** the physical card's signature color.
- **Lesson:** a signature object can become a color identity that survives without the logo.

### Vercel — the triangle
`VC1` · https://vercel.com/ · rendered
- **Source:** the logo's geometry used at architectural scale.
- **Caution:** geometry from a logo works only if the logo geometry is itself distinctive.

### The Pudding — the stories themselves
`PU1 PU2 PU3` · https://pudding.cool/ · rendered
- **Source:** each card's cover is a crop of that story's visualization; hand-lettered wordmark and
  hand-drawn navigation carry a crafted, human voice.
- **Lesson:** an index of content can be varied and recognizable without imposed decoration.

### The Guardian — pillar colors
`GU3` · https://www.theguardian.com/international · rendered
- **Source:** the newspaper's section structure (News, Opinion, Sport, Culture, Lifestyle) encoded as
  a color system used for wayfinding.
- **Lesson:** a color system tied to the product's information architecture is identity *and* function.

### Wikipedia — structure and type
`WPd1 WPm1` · https://en.wikipedia.org/ · rendered
- **Source:** the encyclopedia's structure: serif titles, dense links, hatnotes, language control.
- **Lesson:** plain products can be instantly recognizable through consistent structure.

### Hacker News — density
`HN1 HN2` · https://news.ycombinator.com/ · rendered
- **Source:** its audience's preference for raw lists; the plainness is the identity.
- **Caution:** recognizable is not the same as accessible (small targets, low-contrast metadata).

### Duolingo — the character
`DU1` · https://www.duolingo.com/ · rendered
- **Source:** a mascot with a defined personality used as the focal image; plain actions.
- **Right when:** motivation and habit are the product; the character has a consistent role.

### MDN — the live example
`MD3` · https://developer.mozilla.org/ · rendered
- **Source:** an interactive "Try it" demo, the thing developers came to learn, sits first.

---

## Summary rules

1. List your product's mechanic, signature object, content type, and structure; one of them usually
   holds the identity.
2. Showing the product working (quote, composer, lesson, live demo) beats abstract hero art.
3. Plainness applied consistently is an identity; decoration applied inconsistently is not.
4. A carrier must survive the logo test on sparse and dense pages, not only the hero.
