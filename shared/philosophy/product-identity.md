# Product Identity

Identity is how a product becomes recognizable and trusted as itself. It is built from many
small consistent decisions, not a logo and an accent color.

## Where identity comes from

Derive identity from the product, not from trend boards:

| Source | Questions | Example directions |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | What world does this belong to? What are its materials and artifacts? | Ledger lines for accounting; board geometry for a strategy game; paper and margin for writing |
| Content | What does the product show most? | Photography-led for travel; type-led for editorial; data-led for monitoring |
| Mechanics | What is the core action? | Stacking, matching, routing, scheduling, sorting |
| Audience | Who is here, in what context, on what device? | Classroom projector vs phone on a train vs 27-inch monitor |
| Values | What should the product feel like it believes? | Calm, fast, precise, generous, playful, official |
| Voice | How does it talk? | Plain and direct; warm; technical; witty |

## The layers of identity

1. **Voice and naming.** Terminology, tone, microcopy.
2. **Typography.** Typeface character, scale, weight contrast, how headlines behave.
3. **Color roles.** Not a palette but a system: what color means what.
4. **Shape language.** Corners, strokes, geometry, iconography.
5. **Surface language.** Flat, layered, textured, printed, glowing; how panels relate to the background.
6. **Spatial rhythm.** Density, margins, grid, asymmetry.
7. **Motifs.** Recurring product-derived elements (patterns, marks, characters, shapes).
8. **Imagery and illustration.** Photography style, illustration style, art direction.
9. **Motion language.** How things arrive, respond, and resolve.
10. **Interaction behavior.** Speed, keyboard support, confirmation style, the "feel."

Strong products are consistent across several layers. Weak ones rely on layers 1 and 3 only.

## Identity budget by archetype

Identity intensity should follow the page archetype:

- **Experiential and marketing surfaces:** high. The environment is part of the product.
- **Discovery and commerce:** medium to high around content, restrained at the decision point.
- **Operational, data-heavy, creation tools:** low to medium, expressed through type, rhythm,
  color roles, and interaction feel rather than ornament.
- **Focused and transactional:** identity in the frame and environment; the control itself
  stays plain.
- **Public service:** identity through clarity, consistency, and plain language. Ornament
  usually hurts trust.

## Learning from other products without copying

When referencing another product:

1. Name the specific decision (not "Linear's vibe" but "Linear's dense list rows with muted
   metadata and a single strong status color").
2. Explain why it works *for that product* (its audience, frequency, density).
3. Decide whether your product has the same conditions.
4. Translate the principle, not the surface.

Copying a competitor's surfaces produces a second-rate version of them. Copying the reasoning
produces something that can be your own.
