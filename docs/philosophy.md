# Philosophy

The operational version is `shared/philosophy/experience-core.md`: 29 rules, each phrased as a
behavior, which every skill tells the agent to read first. This page explains where the ideas come
from and how they relate.

## The problem

Agents are now good at producing interfaces that work. They are much weaker at the judgment
around them:

- They mistake alignment for composition and emptiness for minimalism.
- They mistake decoration for identity: gradients, glass, pills, and custom controls instead of
  a product-derived world.
- They stop at "it works" without counting the clicks, questions, and waits.
- They design one perfect screenshot and skip the states around it.
- They animate elements instead of events.
- They praise before evaluating and agree with enthusiasm instead of goals.

## Three commitments

**Make the environment memorable.** A product should feel like a place. Identity comes from the
product's domain, content, mechanics, and audience, expressed through composition, typography,
color roles, motifs, imagery, and motion. It lives mostly in the environment around tasks.

**Make the interaction obvious.** Controls should be familiar, legible, fast, and safe.
Personality applied to a textbox costs usability; applied to the environment it costs nothing.
Do not reinvent familiar interactions without user value.

**Make the outcome fast.** Functionality is the baseline; time-to-outcome is part of quality.
If the software already knows the answer, do not ask. If there is only one option, do not create
a choice. Automate mechanics, preserve judgment. Measure before and after.

## Supporting ideas

- **Empty space must earn its existence.** Space frames, separates, or paces. Otherwise it is dead.
- **Useful density is not filler.** Fix sparse pages with real state and next actions, not fake
  statistics.
- **Real states over screenshots.** Empty, dense, loading, failing, small, zoomed, RTL.
- **Transport is not product.** A failed request is not a failed operation.
- **Motion explains events.** Arrival, transfer, resolution, change; not decoration.
- **Rendered truth beats source.** Look at the result; say what was not verified.
- **Evidence before praise.** Critical is not rude; positive verdicts need reasons too.

## Context is part of taste

No principle here means "always do X." GOV.UK's plain presentation is a strength because
confidence, accessibility, and task completion dominate. Kahoot's full-screen color and
geometry work because a classroom benefits from strong shared visual presence. A government
service should not look like Kahoot; a classroom game should not look like a government form.
Every recommendation in this collection is meant to carry that shape: it works here because…,
it would be wrong when….

## Where it came from

The principles were distilled from repeated, concrete reactions to product work: join pages,
host consoles, dashboards, generators, and flows that were functional but felt empty, generic,
slow, or dishonest. They were generalized so that they apply to websites, apps, games, tools,
commerce, public services, and command-line tools alike.
